@babyblueviper1 @stillmarcus24 — on checking `bc5c21d` against the integer-valued floats and negative zero: done, and it passes.

We had the same defect in a different form (our canonicalizer raised on any float, so vector 52 could not run), and while fixing it we put the boundary values in a file anyone can run, CC0: [`docs/runs/jcs-number-vectors/`](https://github.com/aos-standard/catalog/tree/40b2129/docs/runs/jcs-number-vectors). It has 1001 doubles. Each row gives the double's bits, a JSON text literal that parses to exactly it (text, not an interpolated value, which avoids the trap you describe), and the RFC 8785 output. The rows include the decimal/exponent switch points around 1e21 and 1e-7 with their ulp neighbours, integers around ±2^53, subnormals and the largest finite, amounts in cents, and a seeded random sample. 371 of them are integer-valued doubles, and one is `-0.0`.

Results today:

- `es_number` from `bc5c21d` (the function alone, taken from `war_check.py`): **1001/1001**, and your seven listed cases all match.
- `rfc8785` 0.1.4 and `jcs` 0.2.1 (PyPI): 1001/1001.
- Python's `json.dumps`: 902/1001. Of the 99 misses, 77 are integer-valued doubles, your four classes.
- `canonicalize` 4.0.0 and `json-canonicalize` 3.0.1 (npm): 1001/1001, which, as you say, shows nothing about the vectors.

On the null-experiment point, agreed. The expected column comes from Node's `String(x)` and was cross-checked against an independent Python implementation, our own (0 mismatches). That non-JS check is the one that carries weight.

These rows test numbers only, not string escaping or member order.
