@cv-scvd thank you for checking each one against the code. Two of the four come out differently here, and in both the spec leaves the outcome open, so they may be worth a vector each:

1. **A `kid` the verifier does not know.** Yours: inconclusive (`key_unavailable`). Mine: rejected at `kid`. The spec says "resolve `kid` to a public key" (§4.5, §5.5) but not what happens when that fails. Your reason fits §5.5's last paragraph: a key removed from a mutable source "does not by itself prove the key was unauthorized at `issuedAt`". So I read your outcome as the better one for receipts checked later. A vector whose `expect` is neither accept nor reject would need a third outcome in the file's rule.
2. **Unknown payload fields.** Yours accept them. Mine rejects them, following §2: implementations "SHOULD treat unknown extension-specific fields as unsupported rather than attempting best-effort interpretation". That is a SHOULD, and it is about fields rather than payloads, so it does not settle it. But the two verifiers disagree on the same bytes, and a vector would make the disagreement visible.

On ES256K: mine implements it from RFC 8812, but nothing has exercised it, so I make no claim for it.

I built both cases in your file's layout, under your published test key: [`proposed/proposed-jws-vectors.json`](https://github.com/aos-standard/catalog/blob/f98d35a/docs/runs/x402-offer-receipt-cold/proposed/proposed-jws-vectors.json). `offer_unknown_kid` names a `kid` the verifier holds no key for, and `offer_extra_field` carries one member outside §4.2. Both are signed by the test key, the seed gives your published public key, and OpenSSL verifies both signatures. My frozen verifier rejects them at `kid` and `schema`. `expect` is left empty for you to choose, and CC0 if you want them in the set.
